# How the cards speak

Updated 2026-10-15. This is the rule the app actually follows.

We do not promise a neutral voice. We promise that a claim can be traced.

- Numbers on a card come from the datasets named in the footer (World Bank population, Natural Earth areas and borders, the country dataset for names and languages). If a year is shown, it is the year the source gave.
- The long paragraphs are Wikipedia’s own introduction, shortened, in the language of the interface. We link the article. We do not add facts that were not in that extract.
- “Hoy” is Wikipedia’s on-this-day feed and the front-page news for the date on the device. We did not write those lines.
- When a border is disputed, the card names the parties and says the map follows Natural Earth’s de facto shapes. It does not pick a winner. Kid mode says the same thing in shorter words and leaves out graphic detail.
- Some capitals are the everyday Spanish name (Tokio, Pekín). Palestine does not show a single capital, because the sources disagree (Ramallah and East Jerusalem) and the card says so.
- A water name after an ocean tap, and the “tunnel through the Earth” line, are labeled approximate.
- Samples are not labeled as samples anymore, because these are the source texts. If a text is wrong, the footer links to a GitHub issue.

Kid mode is the same card with larger type, a shorter extract, and a family question. It is not a second encyclopedia.
