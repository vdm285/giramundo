# How the dossier speaks

Updated 2026-10-16. This is the rule the app actually follows.

We do not promise a neutral voice. We promise that a claim can be traced.

- Numbers come from the datasets named in the footer: World Bank population and indicators, Natural Earth areas and borders, Wikidata for places and dishes, the country dataset for names and languages. If a year is shown, it is the year the source gave. A rank ("#104 de 216") counts only countries with a published figure.
- The long paragraphs are Wikipedia's own introduction, shortened at a sentence boundary, in the language of the interface. We link the article. When the Spanish article is missing we show the English one and say so, and the other way around.
- Headlines are the publishers' own titles, linked to the publisher. We collect them from Google News searches and a few world feeds, credit each source, and show when they were collected. We do not summarize, rank by importance, or editorialize. If no feed has anything, the dossier says so.
- "Un día como hoy" is Wikipedia's on-this-day feed for the date on the device. Lines that mention the country come first.
- Weather is Open-Meteo for the capital's coordinates when Wikipedia knows them, otherwise for the country's center, labeled as such. Exchange rates are Frankfurter's daily reference rates. Holidays are Nager.Date's public list.
- Places are UNESCO World Heritage sites as recorded in Wikidata. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons under each file's license, with a link to the file page. Hero photos are the first photograph on the Wikipedia article that is not a flag, a seal, or a map.
- Videos are embedded from YouTube and belong to their creators. We name the channel and link to it. We do not download or re-host anything.
- When a border is disputed, the dossier names the parties and says the map follows Natural Earth's de facto shapes. It does not pick a winner. Crimea falls inside the Russia shape in this file; Laayoune inside Morocco; the cards say so.
- Some capitals use the everyday Spanish name (Tokio, Pekín). Palestine does not show a single capital, because the sources disagree (Ramallah and East Jerusalem) and the card says so.
- A water name after an ocean tap is labeled approximate.
- If a text is wrong, the footer links to a GitHub issue.
